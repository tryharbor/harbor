**Posting one idea on four platforms works, but only if you rewrite it for each one.** The idea stays the same. What changes is the length, the opening line, where the detail goes, and whether the post is text, images or a thread.

Below is one idea taken through all four, then the rules behind each version. The posts are examples written for this article.

## The idea

> When you compare your posts, compare them at the same age. A post that's been up for a week will always beat one that's been up for six hours.

It's a useful idea with a number in it and a mistake most people make. That's enough to work on every platform.

## X: one sharp post, or a short thread

X rewards a first line that stands on its own, because that's most of what people see while scrolling. Free accounts get 280 characters per post.

**Single post:**

> Stop comparing your posts by their totals.
>
> A post that's been up for a week will always beat one that's 6 hours old.
>
> Compare at the same age: 1 hour, 24 hours, 7 days. Then you'll see which posts actually did well.

**If there's more to say, make it a thread** of three to five posts, each of which makes sense on its own. Put the link, if there is one, in the last post or a reply, so the first post is about the idea rather than the click. If you post through X's API, a post with a link also [costs more](/blog/x-api-pricing-what-a-post-costs).

## LinkedIn: a short story with a lesson

LinkedIn gives you 3,000 characters, and readers expect a bit more context: what happened, what you learned, what to do. The feed shows the first two or three lines before "...see more", so those lines have to earn the click.

> I spent a month thinking my Tuesday posts were my best.
>
> They weren't. They were just older.
>
> I was comparing each post's total views, and the posts I'd published earlier in the week had simply had more time to collect them.
>
> When I compared every post at the same age, 24 hours after posting, the pattern flipped. My evening posts beat my morning posts by about a third.
>
> If you're looking at your analytics this week, try it: write down each post's views at 24 hours, take the median, and see which posts beat it.

Short paragraphs, one idea each. No hashtag wall; one to three relevant tags at most, or none.

## Instagram: show it, then explain it in the caption

Instagram needs an image or video, and the post lives or dies on the first slide. A carousel suits an idea with steps.

**Carousel, five slides:**

1. "Your best post probably isn't your best post." (the hook)
2. "Totals favor older posts. A week-old post always wins." (the problem)
3. A simple chart: three posts' views at 24 hours vs their totals. (the proof)
4. "Compare at the same age: 1h, 24h, 7d." (the fix)
5. "Save this for your next analytics check." (the ask)

**Caption:** two or three sentences that add something the slides don't, then a question to invite comments. Links in captions aren't clickable, so point to the link in your bio if you need one.

## Threads: conversational and short

Threads allows 500 characters per post and reads like a conversation. A question or a slightly opinionated take does better than a polished summary.

> Hot take: most "my best post" screenshots are just the oldest post.
>
> Compare at the same age (24 hours is enough) and the ranking usually changes.
>
> Anyone else check this?

You can thread replies under it with the detail, the way you would on X.

## What changes, platform by platform

| | X | LinkedIn | Instagram | Threads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 280 per post (free accounts) | Up to 3,000 | Caption up to 2,200 | 500 per post |
| What carries it | The first line | The first 2 or 3 lines before "see more" | The first image or slide | The first line, in a conversational tone |
| Best format for steps | A short thread | Short paragraphs | A carousel | A short thread |
| Links | Last post or a reply | Fine in the post | Not clickable; use your bio | Fine in the post |
| Visuals | Optional | Optional | Required | Optional |

## A routine that keeps this quick

1. **Write the idea once, as one plain sentence.** If you can't, it isn't ready.
2. **Write the X version first.** The tightest version forces you to find the hook.
3. **Expand to LinkedIn** with the story around it.
4. **Turn the steps into slides** for Instagram.
5. **Loosen it for Threads**, ending on a question.
6. **Spread them out.** Post the versions on different days, so people who follow you in more than one place don't see the same thing four times in an hour.

Then check each version at the same age and see which platform the idea worked best on. That tells you where to post the next one first.

## FAQ

### Can I post the exact same text on every platform?

You can, but it usually does worse than a rewrite. Length limits, how much of the post shows before it's cut off, and whether images are required all differ, so text written for one platform rarely fits another well.

### Which platform should I write for first?

X, or whichever platform has the tightest limit you use. Writing the shortest version first makes you find the hook, and expanding is easier than cutting.

### Should I post all the versions on the same day?

Not necessarily. Spreading them over a few days avoids showing the same idea repeatedly to people who follow you in several places, and gives you a cleaner comparison of how each platform did.

### How many hashtags should I use?

Few or none on X, LinkedIn and Threads. On Instagram, a handful of specific tags is plenty. Hashtags matter much less than the first line or the first slide.
