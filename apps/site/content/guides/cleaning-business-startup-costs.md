## A vacuum: $0 to about $450

You can start with the vacuum you own. If you want a commercial one, a Sanitaire Tradition upright (SC888N) was $445.99 at Quill on November 17, 2026. Commercial uprights are built for daily use and are easy to repair, which matters once you clean several homes a week.

## Insurance and a bond: about $42 a month and up

Clients, especially in their own homes, will ask whether you're insured. Published figures for general liability insurance for a small cleaning business:

- **Insureon:** about $51 a month, or $608 a year, for $1 million / $2 million limits (house cleaners; updated September 2026).
- **Simply Business:** a median of $42 a month, or $504 a year (janitorial; October 2026).
- **Next Insurance:** about half of its janitorial customers pay $38–$50 a month (October 2026).

A **janitorial bond** covers a client if something goes missing. Insureon puts the average at about $112 a year; SuretyBonds.com lists $125 a year for a $10,000 bond and $175 for $25,000 (for businesses with five or fewer employees).

## Registering the business: $0 to a few hundred dollars

If you just start working under your own name, you're a **sole proprietor** automatically, at no cost (SBA). An **EIN** from the IRS is free. An **LLC** is optional, and the filing fee depends on your state: $125 in Florida, $200 in New York and $300 in Texas, plus yearly fees in some states (Florida's annual report is $138.75). Most states don't have a special license for cleaning, but your city or county may require a general business license; check its website.

## Getting the word out: $15 to about $190

A **Google Business Profile** is free and is how most people will find you. **Business cards** were $14.99 for 100 at Vistaprint (with a promotion running), and **yard signs** start at $10.99 each. **Nextdoor** lets you run local ads for under $5 a day, so a month costs about $150.

## What it costs to keep going

After launch, the regular costs are:

- **Insurance:** about $42–$51 a month.
- **Supply refills:** as you use them up.
- **Driving:** the IRS business mileage rate is 72.5¢ a mile for 2026, raised to 76¢ a mile for driving from July 1, 2026. It's a good stand-in for what each mile costs you, and you can deduct it.
- **Card fees:** about 2.6%–3.3% of each payment plus a few cents, depending on the processor and on whether the client pays in person or online (Stripe; Square).
- **Taxes:** self-employment tax is 15.3% of your net earnings, on top of income tax. Set some aside from every job.

## FAQ

### What's the cheapest way to start a cleaning business?

Start as a sole proprietor (free), use your own vacuum, buy a basic kit (about $312), and get liability insurance (about $42 a month). That's under $400 to take your first booking.

### Do I need an LLC to start?

No. You're a sole proprietor by default. An LLC can separate your personal assets from the business, and it costs $125–$300 to file in Florida, New York or Texas. Many owners start without one and form it once the business is steady.

### Do I need insurance before my first job?

It's wise. Most published policies for small cleaning businesses cost $42–$51 a month, and clients often ask. A bond is cheaper still, about $112–$175 a year.
