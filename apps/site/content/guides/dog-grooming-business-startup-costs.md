## Tables, tubs and dryers

- **Budget electric table, $449.99:** a Value Groom 24x36. It raises and lowers, so you aren't bending over every dog.
- **Hydraulic table, $789.99:** a Paw Brothers 48" Hydraulic Z (regular price $939.99). A 48" top fits large breeds. The electric version is $909.99.
- **Stainless tub, $1,889.99:** a Paw Brothers 48" tub with a ramp, so big dogs walk in rather than being lifted.
- **High-velocity dryer:** a Metro Air Force Commander 2-speed ($305.99) or a K-9 III ($615). These blast water out of the coat, which cuts drying time.

## Clippers, blades and shears

- **Clippers:** an Andis AGC2 2-speed with a #10 blade ($148.49, regular $197.99) is a common first clipper. Other workhorses: Wahl KM10+ ($159.99), Oster A5 ($160.59) and Oster A6 3-speed ($194.50). A cordless Andis Pulse ZR II ($324.99) is a useful second clipper for faces and paws.
- **Extra blades:** Andis UltraEdge blades from $26.18 each. Start with the lengths you use most.
- **Shears, $198.99:** a Geib Gator kit with 7.5" straight and curved shears and a thinner.

## Going mobile

- **Trailer, $23,200:** a Hunt's Custom Cargo 7x16 grooming trailer, with a tub, air conditioning and a 50-amp electrical package. A generator isn't included.
- **Grooming van:** builders rarely publish new-van prices. Wag'n Tails, a grooming van builder, lists pre-owned 2023 vans at about **$112,700–$124,200**.

A trailer is far cheaper to start with if you already have a vehicle that can tow it. Wherever you groom, never leave a dog alone in a heated drying cage: New York bans using cage dryers with the heat on, with fines of $250–$500.

## Registration and licenses

You're a **sole proprietor** by default, at no cost, and an EIN from the IRS is free. An **LLC** costs $125 to file in Florida, $200 in New York (plus a publication requirement) and $300 in Texas.

Some states license groomers:

- **Colorado:** a grooming facility license (including mobile groomers) is $400; an independent contractor groomer license is $350.
- **Connecticut:** a grooming license is $200, and a first application needs a zoning official to sign off on your space.
- **Pennsylvania:** no grooming license; you need a sales tax license, because grooming is taxable there.

If you'll groom at home, check your city's zoning rules first. Zoning is set locally, and it can apply to home businesses (SBA).

## Running costs

- **Driving (mobile):** the IRS business rate is 76¢ a mile from July 1, 2026 (72.5¢ before that). It's a fair stand-in for what each mile costs you.
- **Card fees:** about 2.6%–3.3% plus a few cents per payment (Stripe; Square).
- **Self-employment tax:** 15.3% of your net earnings, on top of income tax.

## FAQ

### What's the cheapest way to start a dog grooming business?

Groom in a space you already have, with a budget electric table ($450), a high-velocity dryer ($306), one good clipper ($148), a shears kit ($199), basic supplies and general liability insurance (about $50 a month). That's about $1,420.

### Is a mobile grooming business worth the cost?

It costs far more to start: about $26,200 with a trailer, and over $110,000 for a used van. It saves clients a trip, so price mobile visits to cover the vehicle, fuel and drive time.

### Do I need a license to groom dogs?

It depends on the state. Colorado and Connecticut license groomers; Pennsylvania doesn't. Check your state's agriculture department and your city's business license rules.
