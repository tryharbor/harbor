Most dogs need grooming every few weeks, so a handful of loyal clients quickly fills a calendar. Your first ones will come from people who trust you and from the places dog owners already go. Here's a plan for the first four weeks.

## Week 1: tell everyone you know

Message friends, family, neighbors and old coworkers, especially the dog owners. Ask them to pass it on.

> Hi Alex! I've started a dog grooming business in [your area]: baths, full grooms and nail trims. If you or anyone you know has a dog that needs a groom, I'd love to help. Here's my number: [number].

Your first few clients' dogs are your portfolio. Ask if you can take before-and-after photos.

## Week 1: set up your Google Business Profile

It's free, and it's how people searching "dog groomer near me" find you.

- **Mobile groomers: hide your home address.** Google's rule is that if you don't serve customers at your address, you remove it and show a service area instead.
- **Home salon: show the address** clients come to. If you also travel to clients, you can add a service area too.
- **Add before-and-after photos** and list your services: full groom, bath, nail trim, de-shedding.
- **Ask every client for a review,** with a direct link or QR code.

## Week 2: get to know the vets and pet stores

Dog owners ask their vet and their pet store who they'd recommend. A trade magazine for groomers, Groomer to Groomer, suggests:

- **Introduce yourself to local vets** and leave business cards (100 for about $15).
- **Leave cards at pet stores that don't offer grooming,** and at dog daycares and trainers.

Keep it short: who you are, what you offer, where you work, and a card.

## Week 3: show up where dogs are

- **Offer free nail trims at local events,** like adoption days, pet store events and farmers' markets. It's a few minutes per dog, and owners see your work and take your card.
- **Put magnetic signs on your vehicle,** especially if you're mobile. A parked grooming van on a street is an ad to every neighbor.
- **Try a small Nextdoor ad** for the neighborhoods you want to work in. Nextdoor ads start at under $5 a day.

## Week 4: turn first grooms into regulars

- **Book the next visit before they leave.** "Want me to hold the same time for [Biscuit] in six weeks?"
- **Reward referrals:** "If a friend books a full groom, your next nail trim is on me."
- **Ask for a Google review** with a direct link, sent the same day with a photo of their freshly groomed dog.

## Google's rules worth knowing

- **Use your real business name** on your Google Business Profile. Google doesn't allow extra service words in the name ("Happy Paws Dog Grooming Best Mobile Groomer Tampa"), and profiles that add them can be suspended.
- **Ask for reviews, never pay for them.** Google encourages asking customers to leave a review through a link or QR code, but offering discounts, gifts or free services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. Keep your referral rewards and your review requests separate.

## FAQ

### Do I need a website to get my first grooming clients?

Not for the first few: referrals, vets and your Google Business Profile do most of the work. A simple website helps once people search for your business name, because it shows your services, prices and areas and lets them book.

### Should I offer a discount to win clients?

Reward referrals instead of cutting your price. A referral reward thanks the clients you have and brings in their friends, while a low price is hard to raise later.

### How often do dogs need grooming?

It depends on the coat. Booking each client's next visit before they leave, at the gap their dog needs, is what turns one-off grooms into a full calendar.
