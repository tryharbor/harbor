People buy baked goods they've tasted or that a friend recommends, so your first customers will come from samples, word of mouth and being where people already shop for food. Before you sell, check that your state's cottage food rules allow each place you plan to sell: [every state's rules](/guides/how-to-start-a-home-bakery).

## Week 1: tell everyone you know

Message friends, family, neighbors and coworkers, with photos of what you make.

> Hi Maria! I've started a small home bakery: custom cakes, decorated cookies and sourdough, around [your area]. I'm taking orders for [the holiday] now. Here's my menu: [link]. I'd love to bake for you or anyone you know.

Bring a box to work, a school event or a neighbor. Tasting is what turns people into customers.

## Week 1: set up your Google Business Profile

It's free, and it's how people searching "custom cakes near me" find you.

- **Hide your home address.** Google says that if you don't serve customers at your business address, you should remove it and show a service area instead. That suits most home bakers, who deliver or arrange pickup.
- **List your service areas** by city or zip code, up to 20.
- **Add photos** of your best cakes and cookies, and list what you make.
- **Ask every customer for a review** with a direct link.

## Week 2: take pre-orders

Holidays, birthdays and graduations bring orders in batches. Mississippi State University Extension advises deciding up front whether you'll take **pre-orders** and **digital payments**. Pre-orders let you bake exactly what's sold and take payment before you buy ingredients.

- Post a holiday menu with a **last order date** and set **pickup or delivery days**.
- Take payment when the order is placed.

## Week 3: try a farmers market

A market puts you in front of people who came to buy food. Booth fees vary; Ann Arbor's city market, for example, charges $35 per stall per day.

- **Ask the market first.** Some markets need an extra permit or license even for cottage food sellers (Michigan's agriculture department says so), and in New Jersey vendors need a local temporary food permit.
- **Handle food safely:** University of Minnesota Extension advises covering unpackaged items, using tongs rather than bare hands, keeping stock out of a hot car and off the ground, and labeling everything. Offering open samples there needs a handwashing station.
- **Hand out cards** with your website, so people can order for birthdays later.

## Week 4: talk to local cafes and shops

In some states you can sell to local businesses, which can bring steady weekly orders:

- **New York:** to restaurants, cafes and grocery stores.
- **North Carolina, Georgia and Ohio:** to stores and restaurants (in Georgia, unless your city or county opts out).
- **California:** with a Class B permit.
- **Texas:** to farmers markets, food service and stores that contract with you, for foods that don't need refrigeration.

Other states, including Florida, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Washington and New Jersey, don't allow wholesale. Sell direct there.

## Google's rules worth knowing

- **Use your real business name** on your Google Business Profile. Google doesn't allow extra service words in the name ("Sweet Crumb Bakery Best Custom Cakes Austin"), and profiles that add them can be suspended.
- **Ask for reviews, never pay for them.** Google encourages asking customers to leave a review through a link or QR code, but offering discounts, gifts or free services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. Keep your free samples and your review requests separate.

## FAQ

### Do I need a website for a home bakery?

Not for your first few orders. Once people start ordering for birthdays and holidays, a simple website with your menu, prices, order dates and an order form saves you answering the same questions in messages.

### Can I sell at farmers markets from a home kitchen?

In many states, yes, but check with the market first: some require an extra permit, and New Jersey requires a local temporary food permit.

### Can I sell my baked goods to a coffee shop?

Only in states that allow wholesale, like New York, North Carolina and Georgia, or with a Class B permit in California. Florida, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Washington and New Jersey don't allow it.
