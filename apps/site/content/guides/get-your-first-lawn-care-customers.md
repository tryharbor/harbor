In lawn care, where your clients are matters as much as how many you have. Ten lawns on three streets can be done in a morning; ten lawns across town take all day. Every step below aims for clients close together.

## Week 1: tell everyone you know

Message friends, family and neighbors. Ask them to pass it on, and mention where you're working.

> Hi Jordan! I've started a lawn care business, mowing, edging and cleanups, mostly around [neighborhood]. If you or a neighbor needs a hand this season, I'd love the work. Here's my number: [number].

Your own street and your friends' streets are the easiest place to start a route.

## Week 1: set up your Google Business Profile

It's free, and it's how people searching "lawn mowing near me" find you.

- **Hide your home address.** Google lets businesses that go to their customers show a service area instead.
- **List your service areas** by city or zip code, up to 20. Google doesn't allow a radius, and asks you to stay within about two hours' drive of your base. Keep it far tighter than that, for your route's sake.
- **Add before-and-after photos** of real lawns, and list your services: mowing, edging, cleanups, leaf removal.
- **Ask every client for a review,** with a direct link or QR code. Google doesn't allow rewards for reviews.

## Week 2: let your work advertise itself

A freshly cut lawn is the best ad you have.

- **Yard signs** (from about $11 each at Vistaprint). Ask each client if you can leave one up for a few days after a mow.
- **Business cards** (100 for about $15) to leave with clients and hand to neighbors who stop to chat.
- **Talk to neighbors** while you're working on their street. It isn't a cold call when they can see the lawn you just finished.

## Week 3: reward referrals, not discounts

The National Association of Landscape Professionals warns against cold door-knocking, cold calling and blanket discounts. It recommends **referral credits** and marketing aimed at the neighborhoods you already serve. For example:

> Thanks for having us! If a neighbor signs up for weekly mowing, your next cut is on us.

Run a small **Nextdoor ad** for the same neighborhoods. Nextdoor ads start at under $5 a day, paid 30 days at a time, and you can choose which neighborhoods see them.

## Week 4: lock in the season

- **Offer a regular slot** at the end of every first mow: "I'm on your street every Tuesday. Want me to keep you on the schedule?"
- **Offer the extras early:** a spring or fall cleanup, aeration and leaf removal. They pay more than a mow and keep clients with you all year.
- **Plan for winter.** Mowing is seasonal in most of the country. Some lawn care workers move to snow removal in winter (BLS), and fall leaf cleanups carry you into the cold months.

## Google's rules worth knowing

- **Use your real business name** on your Google Business Profile. Google doesn't allow extra service words in the name ("Green Cut Lawn Care Mowing Experts Dallas"), and profiles that add them can be suspended.
- **Ask for reviews, never pay for them.** Google encourages asking customers to leave a review through a link or QR code, but offering discounts, gifts or free services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. Keep your first-visit offers and your review requests separate.

## FAQ

### Should I go door to door?

Not cold. The landscape trade association advises against cold door-knocking. Talking to neighbors while you're working on their street, and leaving a sign, works with the lawn you've just cut instead of against a stranger's evening.

### Should I offer a discount to win customers?

Offer a referral credit instead of cutting your price. A credit rewards the clients you have and brings in their neighbors, which tightens your route.

### Do I need a website for a lawn care business?

Not for the first few lawns. Once people start searching for your business name, a simple site with your services, areas and a way to request a quote helps you look established and win the next ones.
