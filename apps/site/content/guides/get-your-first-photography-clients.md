People hire photographers from what they can see: your photos, and what friends say about you. So your first job is a portfolio worth sharing, and your second is putting it in front of people who'll pass it on. Here's a plan for the first four weeks.

## Week 1: build a small portfolio

Clients want to see the kind of photos they're booking. Pick the work you want (families, headshots, couples) and photograph a few people in that style: friends, family, colleagues.

- **Get a model release** from everyone you photograph for your portfolio. PPA recommends a signed release before using images for advertising or promotion.
- Show **only your best photos**, not everything you've shot.

## Week 1: tell everyone you know

Message friends, family, neighbors and old coworkers, with a link to your photos.

> Hi Priya! I've started a photography business doing family and portrait sessions around [your area]. Here are a few recent photos: [link]. If you or anyone you know would like photos this season, I'd love to help.

## Week 1: set up your Google Business Profile

It's free, and it's how people searching "family photographer near me" find you.

- **No studio? Hide your address.** Google says service-area businesses should hide their address and show the area they serve instead.
- **Have a studio?** You can show its address if you're able to receive clients there during your stated hours.
- **Add your best photos** and list your sessions: family, portraits, headshots, events.
- **Ask every client for a review** with a direct link.

## Week 2: trade referrals with local businesses

PPA recommends local networking, including trading referrals with other professionals. Good partners send you the people who need photos at the moment they need them:

- **Wedding and event venues and planners** for weddings and parties.
- **Hair and makeup artists** for headshots and portraits.
- **Realtors and local businesses** for headshots and team photos.

Leave them business cards (100 for about $15) and a few examples of your work.

## Week 3: run a mini session day

PPA suggests **themed events with brief sessions** and **early-bird offers** to bring in new clients. Pick a theme (fall leaves, holiday photos, back to school), one location and short time slots, with a set number of edited photos each. You book several families in a day, and each one is a chance for referrals.

Promote it in local Facebook groups (check the rules first) and with a small Nextdoor ad, which starts at under $5 a day.

## Week 4: turn sessions into referrals

- **Deliver quickly** and send the gallery with a thank-you.
- **Ask for a referral:** "If you know anyone who'd like photos, I'd be grateful. I'm booking holiday sessions now."
- **Run an ongoing referral program,** another of PPA's suggestions, like a print credit for every friend who books.
- **Ask for a Google review** with a direct link, sent with the gallery.

## Google's rules worth knowing

- **Use your real business name** on your Google Business Profile. Google doesn't allow extra service words in the name ("Golden Hour Photography Best Family Photographer Denver"), and profiles that add them can be suspended.
- **Ask for reviews, never pay for them.** Google encourages asking customers to leave a review through a link or QR code, but offering discounts, gifts or free services in exchange for reviews is strictly prohibited. Keep your referral rewards and your review requests separate.

## FAQ

### Do I need a website to get photography clients?

A portfolio people can see matters more than anything else, and a simple website is the easiest place for it. It shows your best work, your packages and prices, and lets people book you.

### Should I do free shoots to build a portfolio?

A few, to start, with friends and family who sign a model release. After that, charge, and use mini sessions for lower-priced bookings rather than giving work away.

### Where do photographers find clients?

Mostly through referrals, local search and partners like venues and planners. PPA's advice for finding new clients includes promotions like mini sessions, local networking and a referral program.
