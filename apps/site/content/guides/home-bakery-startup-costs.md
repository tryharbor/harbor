## Equipment

- **Stand mixer:** KitchenAid Artisan 5-quart tilt-head, $349.99 on sale (regular $499.99), or the 7-quart bowl-lift, $499.99 on sale (regular $649.99), for big batches and stiff doughs.
- **Digital scale, $32.99:** an Escali 11 lb scale. Weighing makes every batch the same, and several states require net weight on the label.
- **Pans:** Fat Daddio's 8"x2" round cake pans ($10.49 each, or $9.19 each in sixes) and Nordic Ware half sheet pans ($20.49 each).
- **Cooling rack:** a Wilton 3-tier collapsible rack, $23.99.
- **Decorating:** a cake turntable ($9.19 for an Ateco plastic one, $47.99 for cast iron), a piping tip set ($16.99–$33.49) and disposable piping bags ($9.39–$14.99 per 100).

Equipment prices are from KitchenAid and WebstaurantStore.

## Permits and training

Cottage food permits vary from nothing to a few hundred dollars:

- **Free:** Florida, Texas (for foods that don't need refrigeration), Georgia, Michigan; New York's registration is free.
- **Paid:** Pennsylvania $35 a year, Illinois up to $50 a year (set locally), New Jersey $100 for 2 years, Washington $355 for 2 years. California's fees are set by each county.

A **food handler course** from ServSafe costs $15 online and is valid for 3 years. Illinois and New Jersey require a food protection manager certificate instead, typically $100–$300 (University of Illinois Extension).

## Insurance

- **Food Liability Insurance Program (FLIP):** baked goods insurance from $299 a year, or $25.92 a month, with $1 million per claim and $2 million a year.
- **Insureon:** cake and cookie bakers pay about $39 a month for general liability, or $102 a month for a business owner's policy.

## Running costs

- **Market fees:** for example, Ann Arbor's city farmers market charges $35 per stall per day, or $500 for a year.
- **Card fees:** about 2.6%–3.3% plus a few cents per payment (Stripe; Square).
- **Self-employment tax:** 15.3% of your net earnings, on top of income tax.

## FAQ

### What's the cheapest way to start a home bakery?

Use the mixer and pans you have, start in a state with no permit fee, and buy only a scale, decorating basics, boxes, labels, a $15 food safety course and a month of insurance. That's about $343.

### Do I need a stand mixer?

Not to start. A stand mixer ($350 on sale) saves time once you're making several batches a week, and a bowl-lift model handles bigger batches.

### Do I need insurance for a home bakery?

It protects you if a customer gets sick or has an allergic reaction. FLIP's baked goods policy starts at $299 a year.
