Grooming is priced per dog, by size and coat. A big, double-coated or matted dog takes far longer than a small, short-coated one, so a single flat price loses you money on the dogs that take the most work.

## What people are paying now

Thumbtack's national figures, updated October 28, 2026, put a typical grooming visit at **$95–$160**, with low-end prices around $66 and high-end around $236.

Rover's averages by dog size, seen November 17, 2026:

| Service | Small dog | Extra-large dog |
|---|---|---|
| Full groom | $86 | $211 |
| Bath only | $51 | $138 |

Prices vary a lot by city, so look up three or four local groomers and see what they list. Many post their prices by breed or size.

## Price by size and coat

Set a few size bands (small, medium, large, extra-large) and add to the price for:

- **Long, thick or double coats,** which take longer to wash and dry.
- **Matting,** which can double the time on the table. Tell clients the price up front, and say you'll shave a badly matted coat rather than brush it out.
- **Difficult or anxious dogs,** who need more time and patience.

## Work out your own price

**Price = (your hourly pay × time per dog) + supplies, then add room for card fees.**

An example, with numbers to swap for your own:

- You want to pay yourself **$40 an hour**. For context, the median pay for animal care and service workers was $17.08 an hour in May 2025 (BLS), and as a business you also cover equipment, insurance and the time between dogs.
- A medium dog's full groom takes you **2 hours**: $80.
- Shampoo, conditioner and blade wear, say **$3**: $3.
- Subtotal: $83.
- Card fees (Stripe, 2.9% + 30¢ online): the price needs to be about **$86**.

That's below the $95–$160 typical range. So you can charge $100 or more for a medium full groom and keep a healthy margin.

## Pricing mobile visits

Mobile grooming saves the client a trip and costs you a vehicle, fuel and drive time, so price it higher. Add your driving to the formula:

- **8 miles** to the client at the IRS rate of 76¢ a mile: $6.08.
- **20 minutes** of driving at your $40 hourly pay: $13.33.

In that example, the same medium groom needs about $106 from a mobile client before your trailer or van payment. Book mobile clients on the same streets on the same day to keep driving down.

## Extras worth pricing separately

Nail trims, nail grinding, teeth brushing, de-shedding treatments and de-matting all take extra time. List each as an add-on with its own price, so clients can choose and you're paid for the work. A nail trim on its own makes a good quick booking between full grooms.

## Add sales tax where it applies

Pet grooming is a taxable service in some states, so the tax goes on top of your price. Texas, Pennsylvania and Iowa all tax it. Quote "plus tax" where it applies, and register for a sales tax permit first.

## FAQ

### How much should I charge to groom a small dog?

About $86 for a full groom and $51 for a bath only, on Rover's national averages. Check local prices, which vary by city.

### Should I charge by the hour or by the dog?

By the dog, in size bands, with extra for coat and matting. Clients expect a price per dog. Use an hourly rate only to work out your own minimum, as in the example above.

### How much do card payments cost?

Stripe charges 2.9% + 30¢ online and 2.7% + 5¢ in person. Square's free plan charges 2.6% + 15¢ in person and 3.3% + 30¢ online.
