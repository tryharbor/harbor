There are two numbers to know: what the market pays, and what you need. Charge somewhere they overlap, and lean towards the top of the market once you have reviews.

## What people are paying now

Thumbtack's national figures, updated October 20, 2026:

| Clean | Typical price |
|---|---|
| Standard clean | $146–$202 |
| Deep clean | $190–$265 |
| Move-out clean | $200–$286 |
| Average for a 3-bedroom, 2-bath home | $220 |

Angi, updated October 14, 2026, puts a standard clean at $75–$175 and a deep clean at $200–$400. Move-out cleans average about $360 (range $120–$420), and the hourly rate is $25–$50 per cleaner. With two cleaners, that's about $100 an hour for the team.

Prices vary a lot by city, so look up three or four local cleaners on Google and see what they list.

## Hourly or flat rate?

**Flat rates** are easier for clients: they know the price before you arrive, and you earn more as you get faster. **Hourly** protects you on homes you haven't seen. A common approach: quote the first visit hourly or after a quick walkthrough, then switch to a flat rate for recurring visits once you know the home.

## Work out your own price

A simple formula for one job:

**Price = (your hourly pay × hours) + driving + supplies, then add room for card fees.**

An example, with numbers you should swap for your own:

- You want to pay yourself **$35 an hour**. For context, the median wage for an employed house cleaner was $17.07 an hour in 2025 (BLS), and as a business you also cover taxes, insurance and unpaid time between jobs.
- The job takes you **3 hours**: $105.
- A **10-mile** round trip at the IRS rate of 72.5¢ a mile: $7.25.
- Supplies for the visit, say **$5**: $5.
- Subtotal: $117.25.
- Card fees: Stripe charges 2.9% + 30¢ online, so the price needs to be about **$121** to leave you $117.25.

That's below the $146–$202 going rate for a standard clean. So you don't need to undercut anyone: you can charge $150 and still be well within the market.

## Remember the money that isn't yours

Self-employment tax is **15.3%** of your net earnings (Social Security and Medicare), on top of income tax. The IRS expects estimated payments during the year: for 2026 they're due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15, 2027. Moving a share of every payment to a separate savings account makes those dates painless.

## Recurring clients

Thumbtack's figures show recurring visits priced a little lower than one-time cleans: a visit every two weeks runs about $160–$220, against $174–$256 for a one-time clean. A small discount for weekly or every-other-week clients is normal, and recurring work is what makes the business steady.

## When to raise your prices

Raise prices for new clients once your calendar is mostly full, and tell existing clients a month ahead. Deep and move-out cleans take far longer than standard ones, and HomeGuide notes that a deep clean is often priced at up to double a standard clean, so don't quote them at the same rate.

## FAQ

### How much should I charge for a 3-bedroom house?

Nationally, about $220 on average for a standard clean of a 3-bedroom, 2-bath home (Thumbtack, October 2026). Check local listings, since prices vary by city.

### Should I charge less when I'm starting out?

Not by much. Work out the price you need with the formula above. In the example it was already below the going rate, so underpricing mostly costs you money without winning more clients.

### How much do card payments cost me?

Stripe charges 2.9% + 30¢ online and 2.7% + 5¢ in person. Square's free plan charges 2.6% + 15¢ in person and 3.3% + 30¢ for online payments and invoices.
