Lawn care is priced per visit, by lawn size. Most clients want a flat price they can count on every week, and flat prices reward you for getting faster and keeping your stops close together.

## What people are paying now

LawnStarter's national figures, updated November 4, 2026:

| Lawn size | Per mowing visit |
|---|---|
| Up to ¼ acre | $41–$61 |
| ¼ to ½ acre | $49–$69 |
| ½ to 1 acre | $59–$114 |
| 1 to 2 acres | $99–$264 |
| Over 2 acres | $278–$449 |

Across all lawns, a visit averages about **$57** (range $44–$71), and the hourly equivalent is about **$32–$68**. Many companies set a **minimum of about $30** for lawns under 5,000 square feet.

## Prices vary by region

LawnStarter's weekly mowing prices by region:

| Region | Weekly visit |
|---|---|
| Northeast | $48–$75 |
| Southeast | $44–$68 |
| Midwest | $47–$67 |
| Southwest | $41–$62 |
| West | $43–$69 |

The season length differs too: a longer growing season in the South means more paid visits a year. Look up three or four local lawn services to see what your area charges.

## Extras that pay more than mowing

Seasonal work adds a lot to each client (LawnStarter, October 2026):

| Service | Typical price |
|---|---|
| Lawn aeration | $107–$202 (about $154 on average) |
| Leaf removal | $107–$435 |
| Yard cleanup | $216–$462 |
| Fertilization | $67–$405 |
| Bush trimming | about $46 per bush |

Offer these to your mowing clients first. They already trust you, and a spring and fall cleanup can be worth several weeks of mowing. Check your state's rules before applying any fertilizer or weed killer that counts as a pesticide.

## Work out your own price

**Price = (your hourly pay × time on site) + fuel + driving, then add room for card fees.**

An example, with numbers to swap for your own:

- You want to pay yourself **$40 an hour**. For context, the median pay for landscaping and groundskeeping workers was $18.82 an hour in May 2025 (BLS), and as a business you also cover equipment, insurance and the time between stops.
- A quarter-acre lawn takes you **45 minutes** to mow, trim, edge and blow: $30.
- Mower and trimmer fuel, say **half a gallon** at $3.995 a gallon (EIA, November 2026): $2.00.
- **5 miles** from your last stop at the IRS rate of 76¢ a mile: $3.80.
- Subtotal: $35.80.
- Card fees (Stripe, 2.9% + 30¢ online): the price needs to be about **$37**.

The going rate for a lawn that size is $41–$61. So you can charge $50 and keep a healthy margin, especially once your stops are on the same streets.

## Why route density matters more than price

The less you drive between lawns, the more you earn per hour at the same price. The National Association of Landscape Professionals advises building **route density** with referral credits and marketing to the neighborhoods you already work in, rather than blanket discounts. One well-placed street can hold several clients.

## FAQ

### How much should I charge to mow a quarter-acre lawn?

About $41–$61 a visit nationally (LawnStarter, November 2026), more in the Northeast. Check local prices, and set a minimum charge for very small lawns.

### Should I charge by the hour or by the lawn?

Quote a flat price per visit by lawn size; it's what clients expect. Use an hourly rate only to work out your own minimum, as in the example above.

### How much do card payments cost?

Stripe charges 2.9% + 30¢ online and 2.7% + 5¢ in person. Square's free plan charges 2.6% + 15¢ in person and 3.3% + 30¢ online and for invoices.
