Most new photographers underprice because they count only the hour behind the camera. Choosing the best shots, editing and delivering the gallery often take longer than the shoot. Count all of it.

## What people are paying now

Prices vary a lot by city, so look up three or four local photographers and see what they charge.

## Wedding photography

Couples married in 2025 paid an average of **$3,000** for their wedding photographer, according to The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study of 10,474 couples. Zola puts the national average at **$4,400**, with most couples paying $3,500–$5,300.

Bookings on Thumbtack run much lower, at $525–$1,334 on average, because they're often a few hours of coverage rather than a full day: about $671 for 4 hours and $967 for 6 hours (Thumbtack, October 2026). Price weddings by hours of coverage, and quote a full day as a package.

## Work out your own price

**Price = (your hourly pay × all your hours) + travel + per-session costs, then add room for card fees.**

An example family session, with numbers to swap for your own:

- You want to pay yourself **$40 an hour**. For context, the median pay for photographers was $21.47 an hour in May 2025 (BLS), and as a business you also cover gear, software, insurance and unpaid time.
- **1 hour** shooting, **30 minutes** driving, and **3 hours** choosing, editing and delivering photos: 4.5 hours, or $180.
- **20 miles** round trip at the IRS rate of 76¢ a mile: $15.20.
- Gallery hosting, software and backup for the session, say **$5**: $5.
- Subtotal: $200.20.
- Card fees (Stripe, 2.9% + 30¢ online): the price needs to be about **$206**.

The going rate for a family session is about $269. So you can charge $250 or more and stay within the market.

## Packages, not just hours

Clients find packages easier to choose: a short session with 10 edited photos, a full session with 25, and so on. State the number of edited photos in each package, the delivery time and how they're delivered. Add **prints and albums** as extras; they earn more per client and give people something to hold.

## Mini sessions

Short, themed sessions (fall leaves, holidays, back to school) let you book many families in one day at one location. The Professional Photographers of America (PPA) suggests themed events with brief sessions and early-bird offers to bring in new clients. Price them per session with a set number of photos, so the day pays even with a lower price per family.

## Add sales tax where it applies

Many states tax photography, and the rules depend on what you deliver:

| State | Photography |
|---|---|
| Texas | Taxable |
| New York | Photos delivered only electronically aren't taxable; include prints and the whole charge is taxable (advisory opinion, 2024) |
| Pennsylvania | Taxable when delivered to a Pennsylvania address, physically or digitally |
| Iowa | Taxable, whether the photos are printed or digital |
| Washington | Retail photography is taxable on the total charge |

Quote "plus tax" where it applies, and register for a sales tax permit first.

## FAQ

### How much should I charge for a family photo session?

About $269 on average nationally (Thumbtack, October 2026). Work out your own minimum with the formula above, including editing time, and check local prices.

### How much should a beginner photographer charge?

Work out what you need with the formula above, then compare it with local prices. In the example, a family session needed about $206, below the $269 average, so you don't need to charge rock-bottom prices to win clients.

### How much do card payments cost?

Stripe charges 2.9% + 30¢ online and 2.7% + 5¢ in person. Square's free plan charges 2.6% + 15¢ in person and 3.3% + 30¢ online.
