Dog grooming is steady, repeat work: most dogs need it every few weeks, and owners stay with a groomer they trust. You can start in a space you already have and grow into a salon or a mobile setup. Here are the steps in order.

The same steps apply to any local service business; [how to start a service business](/guides/how-to-start-a-service-business) covers them for every trade.

## 1. Build your skills

Grooming means handling nervous dogs, sharp shears and fast clippers, so skill and safety come first. If you haven't groomed professionally, train first, at a grooming school or under an experienced groomer. Certification isn't required in most places, but it shows clients you've been trained; the National Dog Groomers Association of America now offers membership through AKC Canine College.

## 2. Choose where you'll groom

- **At home or a home salon:** the cheapest start. Clients drop their dogs off. Check your city's zoning rules first, because zoning is set locally and can apply to home businesses (SBA).
- **Mobile:** you go to the client, in a trailer or van. Clients pay for the convenience, but the vehicle costs far more.
- **A rented station in a salon:** you rent space in someone else's salon and clients pay you directly. Colorado licenses this as a "secondary facility."

## 3. Get your equipment

- **Grooming table:** an electric table from $449.99, or a 48" hydraulic table ($789.99) for large breeds.
- **High-velocity dryer:** $305.99–$615.
- **Clippers:** an Andis AGC2 ($148.49) is a common first clipper, plus extra blades from $26.18.
- **Shears kit:** $198.99 for straight and curved shears and a thinner.
- **A tub with a ramp** ($1,889.99) once you set up a home salon.

## 4. Set your prices

Price per dog by size and coat. In late 2026, a typical grooming visit is **$95–$160** (Thumbtack, October 2026). Rover's averages put a full groom at about **$86 for a small dog** and **$211 for an extra-large one**.

## 5. Register the business and check licenses

- **Sole proprietor:** if you start trading under your own name, that's what you are, at no cost (SBA). An **EIN** from the IRS is free.
- **LLC (optional):** $125 to file in Florida, $200 in New York and $300 in Texas.
- **State grooming licenses:** some states license groomers.

| State | Grooming license |
|---|---|
| Colorado | Required (PACFA). $400 for a grooming facility, including mobile groomers; $350 for an independent contractor groomer |
| Connecticut | Required. $200, and a first application needs a zoning official's sign-off |
| Pennsylvania | No grooming license; a sales tax license is required |
| New Jersey | None yet. Bill S387 (2026) would license groomers, require an exam and ban drying cages |

- **Local business license:** your city or county may require a general business license. Check its website.

## 6. Get insured

Ask for coverage for the **animals in your care** (called animal bailee coverage), not just general liability. Insureon's median for groomers' general liability is **$50 a month** ($65 for mobile groomers), and a business owner's policy is about $80. Pet Care Insurance's groomer policy, which includes pet coverage, starts at $26.37 a month. Mobile groomers also need commercial auto insurance: about $245 a month (Insureon).

## 7. Groom safely

Never leave a dog alone on a grooming loop or in a drying cage. New York bans using cage dryers with the heat on, with fines of $250–$500, and New Jersey's pending bill would ban drying cages altogether.

## 8. Set up how you'll get paid and plan for taxes

Clients like paying by card. Card fees run about 2.6%–3.3% plus a few cents per payment (Stripe; Square). Keep business money in its own bank account from the first groom. Self-employment tax is **15.3%** of your net earnings, on top of income tax, so set some aside from every payment.

## 9. Win your first clients

Tell everyone you know, set up a free **Google Business Profile**, introduce yourself to local vets and pet stores that don't groom, and book every client's next visit before they leave.

## 10. Get a simple website

Once people start searching for your business name, a simple website makes you look established. List your services, your prices by size, where you work and a way to book. Harbor can write one from a description of your business, with a form that sends every request to you.

## Check whether you charge sales tax

Some states tax pet grooming. Check yours before you set prices, because the tax goes on top:

| State | Pet grooming |
|---|---|
| Texas | Taxable |
| Pennsylvania | Taxable |
| Iowa | Taxable |

If your state taxes grooming, get a sales tax permit from your state revenue department before your first paid groom.

## Hiring help

When you're ready for a second groomer or a bather, decide first whether they're an **employee or a contractor**. The IRS looks at three things: who controls how the work is done, who controls the money side (tools, expenses, how they're paid), and the type of relationship. Someone you train, schedule and equip is usually an employee.

- **Contractors:** for payments made in 2026, you file a Form 1099-NEC once you've paid someone **$2,000** or more in the year. The threshold was $600 before 2026 (IRS Publication 15).
- **Employees:** workers' compensation insurance is mandatory for most employers in every state except Texas (NAIC). Payroll taxes and withholding apply too.

## Keep business money and records separate

The IRS advises keeping your business bank account separate from your personal one (Publication 583), and the SBA says the same. It makes your income, costs and taxes far easier to see. Keep receipts and records for **at least three years**, and four years for anything to do with employment taxes (IRS).

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to start a dog grooming business?

About $1,420 for a lean start (a budget table, dryer, clippers, shears, supplies and a month of insurance, in a space you already have), about $4,500 for a home salon with a stainless tub and an LLC, and about $26,200 with a mobile grooming trailer.

### Do I need a license to groom dogs?

It depends on your state. Colorado and Connecticut require a grooming license; Pennsylvania doesn't. Your city or county may also require a general business license, and zoning rules apply to grooming at home.

### Do I need to be certified to be a dog groomer?

Not in most places, but training and certification build skill and client trust. The National Dog Groomers Association of America offers membership and certification through AKC Canine College.

### How much do dog groomers charge?

About $95–$160 a visit nationally (Thumbtack, October 2026), with small dogs at the low end and large, long-coated dogs at the high end.
