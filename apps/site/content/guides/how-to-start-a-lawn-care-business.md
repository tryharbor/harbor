Lawn care is one of the most straightforward businesses to start: the work is visible, clients book every week of the season, and you can begin with equipment that fits in a car or truck. Here are the steps in order.


The same steps apply to any local service business; [how to start a service business](/guides/how-to-start-a-service-business) covers them for every trade.

## 1. Choose your services and your area

Start with **mowing, trimming and edging**: the weekly work that brings clients back. Then add the jobs that pay more per visit, like spring and fall **cleanups**, **leaf removal** and **aeration**. Leave chemical weed control and fertilizing until you've checked the licensing rules in step 4.

Pick a **compact area**. The closer your lawns are to each other, the more of your day is paid work instead of driving.

## 2. Get your equipment

You can start small and upgrade as you grow:

- **Mower:** a good self-propelled push mower ($539) for small lawns, a commercial 21" walk-behind ($1,999) for daily use, or a zero-turn ($3,699 and up) once you have larger lawns.
- **String trimmer** ($379.99) and, for a sharper finish, a **stick edger** ($429.99).
- **Ear and eye protection** (about $61).
- A **blower** for clearing clippings, and a **trailer** if your mower won't fit in your vehicle. Get local quotes for both.

## 3. Set your prices

Lawn care is priced per visit by lawn size. In late 2026 a visit averages about **$57**, with **$41–$61** for lawns up to a quarter acre and **$49–$69** for a quarter to half acre (LawnStarter, November 2026). Many services set a minimum of about $30 for very small lawns.

## 4. Register the business and check licenses

- **Sole proprietor:** if you start trading under your own name, that's what you are, at no cost (SBA). An **EIN** from the IRS is free.
- **LLC (optional):** $125 to file in Florida, $200 in New York and $300 in Texas.
- **Licenses:** mowing, trimming and edging usually don't need one. Applying pesticides does: restricted-use pesticides require certification, and many states also certify commercial applicators of other pesticides (EPA). In California, routine gardening doesn't need a state license, but landscape jobs of $500 or more in labor and materials do (C-27).
- **Local business license:** your city or county may require a general business license. Check its website.

## 5. Get insured

General liability insurance covers you if a thrown stone breaks a window or a mower damages a client's property. For lawn care it's about **$57 a month** (Insureon); Next Insurance says most of its lawn care customers pay **$18–$55**. Insurance for your tools and equipment adds about **$42 a month**. If you'll tow a trailer for work, ask your insurer whether your personal auto policy covers it.

## 6. Set up how you'll get paid

Clients like paying by card or invoice, especially for weekly service. Card fees run about 2.6%–3.3% plus a few cents per payment (Stripe; Square). Keep business money in its own bank account from the first job.

## 7. Plan for taxes and the off-season

Self-employment tax is **15.3%** of your net earnings, on top of income tax, so set some aside from every payment. In most of the country mowing is seasonal: plan your year around cleanups and leaf removal in the fall, and consider snow removal in winter, which some lawn care workers take on (BLS).

## 8. Win your first customers

Tell everyone you know, set up a free **Google Business Profile**, leave a yard sign after each job, and reward clients who refer their neighbors. Build your route street by street.

## 9. Get a simple website

Once people start searching for your business name, a simple website makes you look established. List your services, the areas you cover, your "from" prices and a way to request a quote. Harbor can write one from a description of your business, with a form that sends every request to you.

## Check whether you charge sales tax

Many states tax lawn care, including mowing at homes. Check yours before you set prices, because the tax goes on top:

| State | Lawn mowing and care |
|---|---|
| Texas | Taxable. Exempt if you work alone with no employees and have gross receipts of $5,000 or less in the last four quarters |
| New York | Taxable at a home, unless the worker is the homeowner's employee |
| Ohio | Taxable once your sales of the service reach $5,000 a year |
| Pennsylvania | Mowing, fertilizing and edging taxable; landscaping and seeding new lawns not |
| Iowa | Taxable on homes and businesses (snow removal isn't) |
| South Dakota | Taxable, including mowing and trimming |
| Washington | Taxable for homeowners and businesses |

If your state taxes lawn care, get a sales tax permit from your state revenue department before your first paid job.

## Hiring help

When you're ready for a second pair of hands, decide first whether they're an **employee or a contractor**. The IRS looks at three things: who controls how the work is done, who controls the money side (tools, expenses, how they're paid), and the type of relationship. Someone you train, schedule and equip is usually an employee.

- **Contractors:** for payments made in 2026, you file a Form 1099-NEC once you've paid someone **$2,000** or more in the year. The threshold was $600 before 2026 (IRS Publication 15).
- **Employees:** workers' compensation insurance is mandatory for most employers in every state except Texas (NAIC). Payroll taxes and withholding apply too.

## Keep business money and records separate

The IRS advises keeping your business bank account separate from your personal one (Publication 583), and the SBA says the same. It makes your income, costs and taxes far easier to see. Keep receipts and records for **at least three years**, and four years for anything to do with employment taxes (IRS).

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to start a lawn care business?

About $1,050 for a lean start (a push mower, a trimmer, safety gear and a month of insurance, using your own vehicle), about $3,100 with a commercial walk-behind, an edger and an LLC, and about $7,800 with a zero-turn mower.

### Do I need a license to start a lawn care business?

Not usually for mowing, trimming and edging, though your city or county may require a general business license. You do need a state certification to apply many pesticides, and in California landscape jobs of $500 or more need a C-27 license.

### How much can I charge for mowing?

About $57 per visit on average nationally, and $41–$61 for lawns up to a quarter acre (LawnStarter, November 2026). Prices are higher in the Northeast and vary by city.

### What do lawn care businesses do in winter?

Fall cleanups and leaf removal carry the work into late autumn, and some lawn care workers do snow removal in winter (BLS).
