Photography is a business where most people are their own boss: 67% of photographers were self-employed in 2025 (BLS). You can start with one camera and one lens and grow into weddings and events. Here are the steps in order.

The same steps apply to any local service business; [how to start a service business](/guides/how-to-start-a-service-business) covers them for every trade.

## 1. Choose what you'll shoot

Pick one or two kinds of work to start: **family and portraits**, **headshots**, **couples and engagements**, or **weddings and events**. Each needs different gear and a different portfolio, and clients book specialists. Portraits and headshots are the easiest place to start; weddings need more experience and a backup camera.

## 2. Build a portfolio

Clients book from what they see. Photograph friends and family in the style you want to sell, and have each person sign a **model release**: Professional Photographers of America (PPA) recommends one before using images for advertising or promotion.

## 3. Get your gear

- **Camera:** an APS-C Canon EOS R50 ($679.99) to start, or a full-frame Canon EOS R8 ($1,299) for low light.
- **Lens:** a 50mm f/1.8 ($199.99) for portraits; a 24–70mm f/2.8 ($2,399) for events and weddings.
- **Editing:** Adobe Lightroom ($11.99 a month) or the Lightroom and Photoshop plan ($19.99 a month).
- **Delivery and backup:** an online client gallery (free plans from Pixieset, Pic-Time and ShootProof) and cloud backup ($99 a year from Backblaze).

## 4. Set your prices

In late 2026, a session typically costs **$233–$486**, about $164 an hour. Portrait sessions average **$277**, family sessions **$269** and headshots **$304** (Thumbtack, October 2026). Couples paid about **$3,000** for wedding photography (The Knot, 2026). Count your editing time, not just the shoot.

## 5. Register the business

- **Sole proprietor:** if you start trading under your own name, that's what you are, at no cost (SBA). An **EIN** from the IRS is free.
- **LLC (optional):** $125 to file in Florida, $200 in New York and $300 in Texas.
- **Local business license:** your city or county may require a general business license. Check its website.
- **A home studio:** check your city's zoning rules. Zoning is set locally and can apply to home businesses (SBA).
- **Drones:** flying for paid work needs an FAA **Part 107** Remote Pilot Certificate. You must be 16 or older and pass a knowledge test.

## 6. Get insured

Your camera gear is your biggest asset, so insure it along with liability. Insureon's figures for photography businesses: **general liability about $29 a month**, equipment insurance about $43 and professional liability about $42. **PPA's Full membership** ($323 a year) includes up to $15,000 of equipment insurance, and Full Plus ($428) adds $1 million of general liability, except for New York residents.

## 7. Understand your copyright

**You own the copyright to the photos you take,** unless you sign it away in writing (PPA). A client who books a session doesn't own the copyright by default: a commissioned photo is only a "work made for hire" in a few narrow categories, and only with a written agreement signed by both sides (U.S. Copyright Office). Give clients a written license to print and share their photos instead.

Registering your copyright is optional. A group of up to 750 photos costs $55 to register, rising to $85 on January 5, 2027.

## 8. Use a contract for every booking

Put the date, hours, package, price, deposit, delivery time and photo usage in writing before every session. PPA offers contract templates to members, and recommends checking them with a local lawyer.

## 9. Win your first clients

Share your portfolio with people you know, set up a free **Google Business Profile**, trade referrals with venues, planners and other local businesses, and run a themed **mini session** day.

## 10. Get a simple website

Your photos are your best sales tool, and a website is the easiest place to show them. List your sessions, prices and areas, with a way to book. Harbor can write one from a description of your business, with a form that sends every enquiry to you.

## Check whether you charge sales tax

Many states tax photography, and the rules depend on what you deliver. Check yours before you set prices, because the tax goes on top:

| State | Photography |
|---|---|
| Texas | Taxable |
| New York | Photos delivered only electronically aren't taxable; include prints and the whole charge is taxable (advisory opinion, 2024) |
| Pennsylvania | Taxable when delivered to a Pennsylvania address, physically or digitally |
| Iowa | Taxable, whether the photos are printed or digital |
| Washington | Retail photography is taxable on the total charge |

If your state taxes photography, get a sales tax permit from your state revenue department before your first paid session.

## Hiring help

When you're ready for a second shooter or an editor, decide first whether they're an **employee or a contractor**. The IRS looks at three things: who controls how the work is done, who controls the money side (tools, expenses, how they're paid), and the type of relationship. Someone you train, schedule and equip is usually an employee.

- **Contractors:** for payments made in 2026, you file a Form 1099-NEC once you've paid someone **$2,000** or more in the year. The threshold was $600 before 2026 (IRS Publication 15).
- **Employees:** workers' compensation insurance is mandatory for most employers in every state except Texas (NAIC). Payroll taxes and withholding apply too. Photos an employee takes as part of their job belong to your business (PPA).

## Keep business money and records separate

The IRS advises keeping your business bank account separate from your personal one (Publication 583), and the SBA says the same. It makes your income, costs and taxes far easier to see. Keep receipts and records for **at least three years**, and four years for anything to do with employment taxes (IRS).

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to start a photography business?

About $1,135 for a lean start (an APS-C camera, a 50mm lens, memory cards, editing software, backup and a month of insurance), about $4,860 for a full-frame setup with a 24–70mm lens, a flash, PPA membership and an LLC, and about $9,730 for a wedding-ready kit with two bodies.

### Do I need a license to be a photographer?

Usually you need what any business needs: registration, and in some places a general business license from your city or county, plus a sales tax permit where photography is taxable. Flying a drone for paid work needs an FAA Part 107 certificate.

### Who owns the photos, me or the client?

You do, as the photographer, unless you assign the copyright in writing. Give clients a written license to use their photos.

### How much do photographers make?

The median pay for photographers was $44,660 a year, or $21.47 an hour, in May 2025 (BLS). Self-employed photographers set their own prices; sessions typically run $233–$486 (Thumbtack, October 2026).
