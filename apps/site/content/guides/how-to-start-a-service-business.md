A local service business, like cleaning homes, mowing lawns, grooming dogs or taking photos, is one of the fastest ways to work for yourself. You don't need a shop or stock; you need a skill, some equipment and people nearby who'll pay for it. These are the steps in order, with what each one costs. Each links to the detailed guide for a specific trade.

## 1. Pick a service people already pay for

The safest services to start are ones people already buy every week or month: house cleaning, lawn care, pet care. Recurring work means you don't have to find new customers for every job. Look at what's already offered near you. If you see several local cleaners or lawn services on Google, that's a sign of demand, not a reason to stay away.

## 2. Work out what it costs to start

Service businesses are cheap to start because most of the cost is equipment you can buy as you grow. Two examples, priced in November 2026:

| Business | Lean start | Typical start |
|---|---|---|
| House cleaning | about $369 | about $1,196 |
| Lawn care | about $1,052 | about $3,131 |
| Dog grooming | about $1,420 | about $4,500 (home salon) |
| Photography | about $1,135 | about $4,860 |

The lean budgets assume you use your own vehicle and start as a sole proprietor. The typical budgets add commercial-grade equipment, a bond or extra insurance, and an LLC.

## 3. Set your prices

Start from what customers already pay, then check that each job covers your pay, your costs and your taxes. In late 2026, a standard house clean typically went for $146–$202 (Thumbtack, October 2026), a lawn mowing visit for $44–$71 (LawnStarter, November 2026), a dog grooming visit for $95–$160 and a photography session for $233–$486 (Thumbtack, October 2026).

A simple way to price one job: **your hourly pay × hours, plus driving and supplies, then add room for card fees.** The IRS business mileage rate (76¢ a mile from July 1, 2026) is a fair stand-in for what driving costs you. In both trades, this formula comes out below the going rate, so new owners rarely need to undercut anyone.

## 4. Register the business

- **Sole proprietor.** If you start working under your own name without registering anything, you're a sole proprietor automatically (SBA). It's free, and you report the income on your personal tax return.
- **EIN.** An Employer Identification Number from the IRS is free. It's useful for opening a business bank account and for paying anyone later.
- **LLC (optional).** It keeps business debts separate from you. Filing costs vary by state: $125 in Florida, $200 in New York (plus a newspaper publication requirement) and $300 in Texas, and some states charge yearly fees. You can start as a sole proprietor and form an LLC later.
- **Local business license.** Many cities and counties require a general business license. Your city or county website will say.

## 5. Check the rules for your trade

Most service businesses don't need a special state license, but some kinds of work do:

- **Pesticides and weed control:** anyone who applies restricted-use pesticides must be certified, and many states also certify commercial applicators of other pesticides (EPA).
- **Landscaping in California:** routine mowing and trimming doesn't need a state license, but landscape jobs of $500 or more in combined labor and materials need a C-27 contractor's license.
- **Janitorial work in California:** there's a janitorial registration with the Labor Commissioner, so check whether it applies to you.

## 6. Get insured

Liability insurance covers damage you might cause in a customer's home or yard, and customers often ask about it. Published figures for 2026:

| Business | General liability, per month |
|---|---|
| House cleaning | about $42–$51 (Simply Business; Insureon) |
| Lawn care | about $57 (Insureon); most Next Insurance customers pay $18–$55 |
| Dog grooming | about $50 (Insureon); $65 for mobile groomers |
| Photography | about $29 (Insureon) |

Cleaners often add a janitorial bond (about $112–$175 a year), and lawn care businesses often insure their tools and equipment (about $42 a month).

## 7. Set up how you'll get paid

Card payments make it easy for customers to pay on the day or by invoice. Stripe charges 2.9% + 30¢ online and 2.7% + 5¢ in person; Square's free plan charges 2.6% + 15¢ in person and 3.3% + 30¢ online and for invoices. Keep business money in its own bank account from the first job. It makes taxes much easier.

## 8. Plan for taxes

When you work for yourself, nobody withholds tax from your pay:

- **Self-employment tax** is 15.3% of your net earnings (12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare), on top of income tax (IRS).
- **Estimated payments** are due during the year. For 2026: April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15, 2027.
- **Set aside a share of every payment** in a separate account, so the due dates aren't a shock.

## 9. Get your first customers

- **Tell everyone you know,** and ask them to pass it on. People who already trust you are the easiest first customers.
- **Set up a free Google Business Profile.** Service businesses can hide their home address and list up to 20 service areas, within about two hours' drive. It's how people searching "near me" find you.
- **Ask every customer** for a review (Google doesn't allow rewards for reviews) and for a referral.
- **Try a small local ad.** Nextdoor ads start at under $5 a day.

You can win your first few customers without one. Once people start searching for your business name, a simple website shows you're real. ## FAQ

### How much does it cost to start a service business?

It depends on the equipment. A house cleaning business can start for about $369 and a lawn care business for about $1,052, using a vehicle you already have and starting as a sole proprietor (prices checked November 2026).

### Do I need an LLC to start a service business?

No. You're a sole proprietor by default, at no cost. An LLC separates business debts from you and costs $125–$300 to file in Florida, New York or Texas, plus yearly fees in some states.

### Do I need a license to start a service business?

Usually not a special state license for cleaning or mowing, but many cities and counties require a general business license. Some work, like applying pesticides, does need state certification.

### What taxes do I pay when I'm self-employed?

Income tax plus self-employment tax of 15.3% of your net earnings, paid through estimated payments during the year. For 2026 they're due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15, 2027.

### Which service business is cheapest to start?

Of the two priced here, house cleaning: about $369 for a lean start, against about $1,052 for lawn care, because cleaning supplies cost far less than a mower and trimmer.
