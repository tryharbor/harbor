## Mowers: about $540 to $12,000

- **Push mower, $539:** a Toro 22" Recycler with Personal Pace self-propel (model 21465). It's fine for small suburban lawns while you build a route.
- **Commercial 21" walk-behind, $1,999:** an Exmark Commercial 21 X-Series (cash price; listed at $2,213.47). It's built for daily use and is worth having even later, for gated yards and tight spots.
- **Entry zero-turn, $3,699:** a Toro 42" TimeCutter (model 77404), sold through dealers. A zero-turn cuts large lawns far faster.
- **Commercial zero-turn, about $11,500:** an Exmark Lazer Z E-Series 60" (cash price $11,549, listed at $12,049.97). Most people starting out don't need one.

All four prices are from the manufacturers' own pages on November 17, 2026. Dealers often run promotions, and some brands add tariff surcharges, so ask for an out-the-door price.

## Trimmer, edger and safety gear: about $440 to $870

- **String trimmer, $379.99:** a Stihl FS 91 R, a professional trimmer.
- **Stick edger, $429.99:** a Stihl FC 91. Crisp edges along drives and paths are what make a lawn look professionally done.
- **Protection, about $61:** Stihl earmuffs ($37.99) and clear safety glasses ($22.99). Mowers and blowers are loud enough that you'll want hearing protection every day.

A **backpack blower** for clearing clippings is usually the next buy. Prices vary widely by model, so check your local dealer.

## Insurance: from about $57 a month

Published figures for lawn care businesses:

- **General liability (Insureon):** about $57 a month, or $685 a year. It covers damage to a client's property, like a stone thrown through a window. Next Insurance says most of its lawn care customers pay $18–$55 a month.
- **Tools and equipment:** about $42 a month (Insureon), or $31–$64 (Next Insurance). It covers your gear if it's stolen from a trailer or truck.
- **Commercial auto:** if you use a truck and trailer for work, check with your insurer whether your personal policy covers it. Insureon's lawn care customers pay about $255 a month for commercial auto.

## Registration and licenses

You're a **sole proprietor** by default, at no cost, and an EIN from the IRS is free. An **LLC** costs $125 to file in Florida, $200 in New York (plus a newspaper publication requirement) and $300 in Texas.

**Mowing itself usually doesn't need a license, but some work does:**

- **Pesticides:** applying restricted-use pesticides requires certification, and many states also certify commercial applicators of other pesticides (EPA). Check your state before you offer weed control or fertilizing with chemicals.
- **California:** gardeners doing routine mowing, weeding and trimming generally don't need a state license. Landscape jobs of $500 or more in combined labor and materials do (the C-27 license).

## Running costs

- **Fuel:** regular gasoline averaged $3.995 a gallon in the week of November 9, 2026 (EIA).
- **Driving:** the IRS business mileage rate is 76¢ a mile for driving from July 1, 2026 (72.5¢ before that). It's a fair stand-in for what each mile costs you.
- **Card fees:** about 2.6%–3.3% plus a few cents per payment (Stripe; Square).
- **Self-employment tax:** 15.3% of your net earnings, on top of income tax.

## FAQ

### What's the cheapest way to start a lawn care business?

Use a vehicle you already have, buy a good push mower ($539) and a trimmer ($380), wear ear and eye protection, and get liability insurance (about $57 a month). That's about $1,050.

### Do I need a zero-turn mower to start?

No. A push mower or a commercial 21" walk-behind handles most suburban lawns. A zero-turn ($3,699 and up) makes sense once you have larger lawns or a full route.

### Do I need a license to mow lawns?

Usually not for mowing, trimming and edging. You do need a state certification to apply many pesticides, and in California landscape jobs of $500 or more need a contractor's license.
