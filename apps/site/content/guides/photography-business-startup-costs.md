The camera and lenses are most of the cost, and what you need depends on what you'll shoot. Portraits and headshots need far less than weddings, where a backup camera is a must. Below is what everything costs, with prices seen on November 17, 2026. Many camera prices are holiday sale prices; regular prices are shown alongside.

Add a spare battery for whichever body you buy, and a computer if yours can't handle editing.

## Cameras: about $680 to $2,000

- **Canon EOS R50, $679.99** (regular $779.99): an APS-C camera, good enough for portraits and headshots while you build a portfolio. The Canon EOS R10 is $1,099.
- **Canon EOS R8, $1,299** (regular $1,649): an entry full-frame camera, better in low light, which matters indoors and at events.
- **Nikon Z5II, $1,599.95** (regular $1,849.95): Nikon's entry full-frame.
- **Sony a7 IV, $1,998** (regular $2,498), at an authorized Sony dealer.

For weddings, carry **two bodies**. If a camera fails mid-ceremony, there's no second chance.

## Lenses: start with one good prime

- **Canon RF 50mm F1.8 STM, $199.99** (regular $239.99): the classic first portrait lens, with a soft background at a low price.
- **Canon RF 24–70mm F2.8L, $2,399** (regular $2,599): the everyday event and wedding lens.
- **Canon RF 70–200mm F2.8L Z, $3,099** (regular $3,299): for ceremonies and candids from a distance. The F4 version is $1,549.
- **Flash:** a Canon Speedlite EL-10 ($239.99) or the pro EL-5 ($349.99), for dark venues and fill light.

Lens prices are from Canon's store; check Nikon's or Sony's for their lenses.

## Insurance: from about $29 a month

- **General liability (Insureon):** about $29 a month, or $350 a year, for photography businesses. It covers a client tripping over your light stand.
- **Equipment insurance (Insureon):** about $43 a month. It covers stolen or damaged gear, usually your biggest asset.
- **Professional liability (Insureon):** about $42 a month. It covers claims of a mistake, like a missed wedding moment.
- **Full Frame Insurance:** liability from $129 a year, or $347 a year with camera equipment, and $59 for a 1–3 day event policy.
- **NEXT Insurance:** it says 75% of its photographer customers pay about $19 a month on average for general liability.

**PPA membership** includes insurance: the Full plan ($323 a year) covers up to $15,000 of equipment, and Full Plus ($428 a year) adds a $1 million general liability policy. Neither insurance benefit is available to New York residents. The typical budget pairs PPA Full with a month of Insureon liability; the wedding-ready budget uses Full Plus.

## Registration, copyright and drones

- **Sole proprietor** by default, at no cost, and an EIN from the IRS is free. An **LLC** costs $125 in Florida, $200 in New York (plus a publication requirement) and $300 in Texas.
- **Copyright registration** is optional. A group of up to 750 photos costs **$55** to register today, rising to **$85 on January 5, 2027** (U.S. Copyright Office).
- **Drones:** flying for paid work needs an FAA **Part 107** Remote Pilot Certificate. You must be 16 or older and pass a knowledge test at an FAA-approved testing center.
- **A home studio:** check your city's zoning rules first. Zoning is set locally and can apply to home businesses (SBA).

## Running costs

- **Driving:** the IRS business rate is 76¢ a mile from July 1, 2026 (72.5¢ before that).
- **Card fees:** about 2.6%–3.3% plus a few cents per payment (Stripe; Square).
- **Self-employment tax:** 15.3% of your net earnings, on top of income tax.

## FAQ

### What's the cheapest way to start a photography business?

Start with an APS-C camera ($680) and a 50mm lens ($200), two memory cards, Lightroom ($12 a month), a free client gallery plan, cloud backup ($99 a year) and liability insurance (about $29 a month). That's about $1,135.

### Do I need a full-frame camera?

Not for portraits and headshots in good light. A full-frame camera helps indoors and at events, where light is low. For weddings, two bodies matter more than which ones.

### Is PPA membership worth it?

For many new photographers, yes: the Full plan ($323 a year) includes up to $15,000 of equipment insurance, plus contracts, education and a referral listing. Check that you're eligible for the insurance (it excludes New York).
