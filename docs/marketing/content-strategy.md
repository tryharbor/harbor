# Content strategy: be the source for starting a local service business

Goal: when someone asks Google, ChatGPT or Perplexity how to start a business (a cleaning business, a lawn care business,
a home bakery), Harbor's pages are among the sources cited, and the reader meets a product that does the next step for
them. Audience: people starting a local service business. Written 2026-11-17 from two research passes (who ranks and
gets cited today; what the studies say earns citations). Copy rules: docs/marketing/copy-guide.md.

## What the research says

**Who wins today**

- **"How to start a business" and "steps to starting a business"** belong to SBA, Shopify, LegalZoom and formation
  companies. A new site won't win these head-on soon.
- **Trade questions** ("how to start a cleaning business", "how much does it cost to start…", "how to price…") are spread
  across mid-sized vendor blogs, and no one owns them. The ones that keep showing up are:
  - **Jobber and Housecall Pro** (field-service software): our real content competitors.
  - **MoeGo**, for grooming.
  - **Durable**, which already has 14 trade guides: detailed, but very long, dated December 2025, with no original data,
    and with gaps (grooming, photography, baking).
- **Nobody publishes:**
  - itemized, sourced, dated startup budgets per trade;
  - real pricing formulas and local rate ranges;
  - a week-by-week plan for the first ten customers;
  - local search for a one-person business;
  - getting paid (deposits, invoices, late payers);
  - a neutral answer to "do I need an LLC" (formation sellers own it).

**What earns citations** (the evidence is mostly correlation; one controlled study, the GEO paper)

- **Specific numbers, quotes from real people and cited sources** raised visibility in the one controlled experiment.
  The biggest gains went to lower-ranked pages, which is us.
- **Being mentioned around the web** (Reddit, YouTube, other sites) correlates with AI visibility far more than
  backlinks do. YouTube is the most-cited domain in Google's AI Overviews.
- **Engines cite pages that don't rank for the main question.** Google expands each question into sub-questions
  (costs, LLC, pricing, first customers), and pages that answer those get cited. So winning "how to start a business"
  runs through its sub-questions.
- **Freshness counts**, most of all for ChatGPT and Perplexity. Pages updated in the last three months get cited
  noticeably more.
- **Answer first.** A large share of citations come from the top third of a page.
- **Depth helps:** roughly 120–180 words per section, and many specific data points.
- **What doesn't help:** llms.txt (engines barely fetch it), FAQ or schema markup as a citation lever, question-style
  headings, and keyword-stuffed titles.

## The plan

### 1. Trade guides, five trades first

Cleaning, lawn care / landscaping, dog grooming (mobile and home), photography, and home bakery (cottage food). They
cover high demand plus the trades no one covers well. Each trade gets a small set of pages:

- **Start:** how to start a [trade] business in 2026.
- **Cost:** what it costs, with an itemized lean / typical / full budget. Real prices, sourced and dated. This is the
  page people will quote.
- **Price:** how much to charge, with a worked formula and real rate ranges, sourced.
- **First customers:** a week-by-week plan to get the first ten, with the actual messages to send.

### 2. Questions every trade shares

Neutral, specific and short:

- LLC or sole proprietor for a service business.
- Google Business Profile when you work from home (service-area businesses).
- Getting paid: deposits, invoices, card fees, no-shows.
- Your first 30 days.
- Do you need a website, and what goes on it.
- Taxes in year one (estimated payments, keeping business money separate).

### 3. A hub that ties it together

"How to start a service business" links every trade and shared page. It answers the big question briefly and sends
each sub-question to its own page. This is the page that gets cited once the sub-pages are.

### 4. Original data, refreshed twice a year

- **Service business startup cost index:** a dated, itemized cost table for each trade, built from public retailer and
  licensing prices with sources. Something others can quote.
- **Later, once there's an audience:** a short survey of real owners (what they charge, what it cost, how they found
  their first customers).

### 5. Free tools on the guide pages

A startup cost calculator and a "what should I charge" calculator, each ending in "see your website" (Harbor's website
builder on a small scale). These get linked and mentioned, and they fill the early-access list.

### 6. Off-site, where engines look

- **YouTube walkthroughs:** "starting a cleaning business: day one", built in Harbor on screen.
- **Genuinely useful answers on Reddit**, in places like r/sweatystartup and r/smallbusiness. No link-dropping.
- **Owner interviews:** they give real quotes for the guides, and owners share pages that feature them.

## How every page is written

- **Opening:** a 40–60 word direct answer, then sections of about 150 words under plain headings.
- **Facts:** real numbers with a source and a date. Quotes come from owners we actually spoke to. Never invent a quote,
  a statistic or a testimonial.
- **Dates:** a visible "Updated" date, with a real refresh every quarter.
- **Voice:** the copy guide's rules. Outcomes, plain words, "you". No hype titles ("in 5 minutes").
- **Length:** shorter than Durable's 33-minute guides, and deeper on the numbers.
- **The Harbor step:** one natural line near the end where it actually helps ("Describe your business and Harbor
  builds the website"). It's never the point of the page.

## Measuring it

- **Every month:** run a fixed list of about 30 questions in Google, ChatGPT and Perplexity and record who gets cited.
  Harbor's citations are the main number.
- **Also track:** Search Console impressions for the guide pages, early-access sign-ups from guide pages, and mentions
  on Reddit and YouTube.

## What we stop

- **No new social-scheduler content** (Buffer vs Hootsuite, X API costs). The existing posts stay up.
- **No llms.txt or schema work** done for AI citations, since the evidence says it doesn't move them.
