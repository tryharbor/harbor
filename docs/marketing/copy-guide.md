# Harbor copy guide

How Harbor's site and marketing should sound, from a study of competitors' homepages on 2026-11-17: Lovable, Bolt,
Replit, Base44 and v0 (AI app builders), and Durable, Wix, Squarespace, Shopify, Stan and Hostinger (small-business and
creator tools). Harbor is one place to build the business (a website or a real app), get customers (posts written for
you) and get paid (see the money come in).

## The rules

1. **Sell the outcome, not the mechanism.** The best heroes promise a result or an identity ("makes it real", "make
   money online", "bring an entire company to life"). Nobody opens with how it works. Ours: a running business, not an
   app, a site or an AI.
2. **Proof and safeguards support; they never lead.** No competitor's hero mentions approvals, evidence, errors or
   risk. One short reassurance line near the button is the most they do ("you can always make changes later"). Approvals,
   code ownership, model choice and costs belong in FAQs and deep pages.
3. **Talk about money plainly.** The verbs that work for owners are about getting paid and found: sell, get booked,
   get paid, find customers. Money outcomes ("made its first $X") persuade more than logos our audience can't relate to.
4. **Name real people and real things.** "A booking page for a dog groomer" beats "apps and websites". Specific trades
   and specific projects let a visitor see themselves.
5. **Say "you" and "your business".** Short sentences. Rhythmic pairs and threes, used sparingly. Plain words a
   first-time owner uses; no "stack", "agent", "MCP", "sandbox", "model", "deploy" above the fold.
6. **AI is a helper, not the headline.** Competitors either make it a friendly partner or barely mention it. We say what
   it does for you ("writes your posts", "builds what you describe"), never how.
7. **Count what we replace.** "All-in-one" is a cliché unless it's concrete: name the tools a new business ends up
   juggling.
8. **Never claim what isn't built.** Coming features say "coming" or stay off the page. No invented numbers or
   testimonials; we have none yet, so we use real examples instead.

## Where Harbor is different (the gaps no one fills)

- **Life after launch.** Every builder stops at "publish". Nobody sells getting customers or knowing whether money is
  coming in. Harbor does all three in one place.
- **A real app, in plain language, for non-technical owners.** Builders speak to "builders"; website tools stop at
  sites. "When the product is software, build that too" is ours to say first.
- **"Is it working?"** Your sales next to your marketing, without a spreadsheet.
- **Your business, not your app.** Everything starts from one description of the business, so nothing is typed twice.

## Words to avoid

Clichés: "What will you build?", "from idea to app", "in minutes", "superpowers", "builders", "no code needed",
"stunning", "endless possibilities", "the whole stack", "all-in-one" (bare), "unfair advantage", "grow your business
faster", "dream big", "world's best", urgency banners.

Old Harbor language to retire from headlines: "content strategist", "Brand Memory" (now Business Memory, and only
inside the product), "evidence", "every rule linked to", "nothing posts without asking", "baseline", "growth curves",
"per brand", "MCP", "open source".
